Here are the exact fields of mathematics that study these stratified, multi-arity relations and their induced constraints:
──────
### 1. Simplicial Topology and Simplicial Sets (Topological Combinatorics)

• What it studies: Abstract simplices where points (0-simplices / Unary elements) are joined by lines (1-simplices / Binary edges), bounded by triangular faces (2-simplices / Ternary
relations), and filled by tetrahedra (3-simplices / Quaternary relations).
• Key Concepts to search:
• Face and Degeneracy maps (∂ᵢ, σᵢ): Directly model the projections between higher and lower arities.
• Abstract Simplicial Complexes and Delta-complexes: Hyper-structures where every face of a relation must itself belong to a lower-order relation.
• Kan Complexes and Horn-filling conditions: The exact topological rules governing whether partial paths in lower arities can be completed by higher-order relation units.

──────
### 2. Higher Category Theory and Fibrations

• What it studies: Hierarchies of objects (0-cells), morphisms between objects (1-cells), and morphisms between morphisms (2-cells), organized into n-categories or double categories.
• Key Concepts to search:
• Grothendieck Fibrations: Formalizes a structure "fibered" over another structure, where the top layer strictly projects to and enforces invariants on the base layer (the mathematical
basis of T ⟹ B ⟹ U).
• Internal Categories and Double Categories: Categories whose objects and arrows are themselves structured sets satisfying commutativity and composition.
• Operads and Multicategories: Structures where relations take multiple inputs from different sorts and produce a single output identity.

──────
### 3. Polyadic Formal Concept Analysis (Triadic & Multimodal FCA)

• What it studies: Discrete mathematics of k-ary incidence relations across k distinct sets (formal contexts) and the lattices of closed structures they generate.
• Key Concepts to search:
• Triadic Concept Analysis (TCA) (introduced by Rudolf Wille): Studies ternary relations I ⊆ A × B × C and their concept trilattices.
• Galois Connections and Closure Operators: The mathematical foundation of Theorem 6 (structural causation, where relations across arities derive and close element sets).
• Multimodal Concept Lattices: Cross-domain lattices where attributes enforce implications on object sets.

──────
### 4. Multipartite Directed Hypergraph Theory

• What it studies: Graph theory generalized to edges connecting subsets across k disjoint vertex partitions (k-partite hypergraphs).
• Key Concepts to search:
• Incidence Geometries and Linear Hypergraphs: Hypergraphs where hyperedges intersect in at most one vertex or pair.
• Acyclic Hypergraphs (α-acyclicity, β-acyclicity, γ-acyclicity): Rigorous topological classifications of acyclicity and tree-likeness in hyper-relations.
• Polymatroids and Oriented Matroids: Combinatorial abstractions of linear independence and acyclic orderings across multi-set systems.

──────
### 5. Finite Model Theory and Multi-Sorted Relational Structures

• What it studies: Logic and relational structures with multiple domains (A, B, C) subject to first-order integrity constraints and dependencies.
• Key Concepts to search:
• Multi-Sorted First-Order Logic: Formal logic where variables belong to distinct types/arities.
• Inclusion and Embedded Dependencies (EGDs and TGDs): Formal database theory rules where the existence of a tuple in a higher relation forces relations or equalities in lower domains.
• Homomorphism Dualities and CSP Cores: Classifying which topological restrictions (e.g. DAG, tree, bipartite) preserve tractable algorithmic properties.

──────
### Recommended Starting Point

• For hierarchical containment and arity reification (T ⟹ B ⟹ U): Read Simplicial Sets and Grothendieck Fibrations.
• For structural causation and derived concepts (Theorem 6): Read Triadic Concept Analysis (Wille & Lehmann).
• For acyclicity and tree restrictions on hyper-relations: Read Hypergraph Acyclicity (α/β/γ).
